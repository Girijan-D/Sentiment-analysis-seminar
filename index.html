01 — FOUNDATION

Why do we need these techniques?

Modern systems generate enormous amounts of sequential information. Tweets, customer reviews, stock prices, weather measurements, medical signals and website traffic all contain patterns that change with time or context.

Human Language

Text contains opinions, emotions, intentions and attitudes. Sentiment analysis attempts to extract this information automatically.

Temporal Data

Time series data records observations in chronological order. The previous values can provide information about future values.

Sequential Learning

RNNs are designed to process sequences while maintaining information about previous elements.

02 — NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING

Sentiment Analysis

Sentiment analysis is the computational process of identifying subjective information in text and determining the attitude, emotion or polarity expressed by the writer.

Core idea: Instead of simply asking: "What words are present?" we ask: "What does the text express?"

What can a sentiment system detect?

Positive

"The camera quality is incredible."

Negative

"The battery died after only two hours."

Neutral

"The phone has a 6.7-inch display."

Emotion

Joy, anger, fear, sadness, surprise, etc.

Step 1 — Text Preprocessing

Tokenization

Breaking text into smaller units such as words or subwords.


"I love this phone" → ["I","love","this","phone"]

Lowercasing

Converting text into a consistent representation.


"AMAZING" → "amazing"

Stop-word Handling

Words such as "the", "is" and "a" may sometimes be removed, depending on the model and task.

Stemming / Lemmatization

Reducing related words to a common representation.

Feature Extraction Techniques

Technique Idea Advantage
Bag of Words Represents text using word occurrence counts. Simple and interpretable.
TF-IDF Weights words according to their importance. Good traditional baseline.
Word Embeddings Represent words as dense numerical vectors. Captures semantic relationships.
Contextual Embeddings Representation changes according to context. Better understanding of language.

Major Sentiment Analysis Approaches

1. Lexicon-Based

Uses a dictionary containing words associated with sentiment.


"excellent" → positive
"terrible" → negative


Advantage: Easy to implement.

Disadvantage: Struggles with context, sarcasm and negation.

2. Machine Learning

Algorithms such as Naive Bayes, Logistic Regression, SVM and decision trees learn sentiment patterns from labelled data.


Advantage: Better than simple word counting.

Disadvantage: Requires labelled training data.

3. Deep Learning

Neural networks learn representations automatically from large datasets.


CNNs, RNNs, LSTMs and Transformers can be used.

Advantage: Captures complex patterns.

Disadvantage: Requires more data and computation.

Interesting Real-World Application

Imagine a major smartphone launch.

Within hours, millions of people post:

"Camera is insane 🔥"
"Battery is disappointing."
"Best phone I've ever used."
"The design is beautiful but overheating is horrible."

A company cannot manually read millions of posts. Sentiment analysis can process them and create a live perception map of the product. It can reveal that customers love the camera but hate the battery, allowing the company to prioritize engineering improvements.

Advantages

  • Processes huge volumes of text automatically.
  • Useful for real-time monitoring.
  • Can reveal customer preferences.
  • Supports business and decision-making systems.
  • Can detect changes in public opinion.

Disadvantages

  • Sarcasm is difficult to interpret.
  • Negation can change meaning.
  • Language can be ambiguous.
  • Different domains require different training data.
  • Bias in training data can produce biased predictions.

04 — DEEP LEARNING

Recurrent Neural Networks

A Recurrent Neural Network, or RNN, is a neural network designed to process sequential data by maintaining a hidden state that carries information from previous time steps.

The key idea:

An ordinary neural network asks:
"What is the current input?"

An RNN asks:
"What is the current input + what have I learned from previous inputs?"

Why do we need RNNs?

Consider the sentence:

"The movie was not good because the acting was extremely..."

The earlier words influence what comes next. A sequential model needs some form of memory.

RNN Training

RNNs are commonly trained using Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT). The network is conceptually unrolled across time and the error is propagated backward through those time steps.

The Vanishing Gradient Problem

During BPTT, gradients may become extremely small as they are propagated through many time steps. When this happens, the network has difficulty learning long-term dependencies.

Solution: Architectures such as LSTM and GRU were developed to handle long-term dependencies more effectively.

RNN Variants

Architecture Main Idea
Simple RNN Basic recurrent hidden state.
LSTM Uses gates and memory cell to retain useful information.
GRU Simplified gated architecture with fewer components.

Advantages

  • Designed for sequential data.
  • Can use information from previous time steps.
  • Works with variable-length sequences.
  • Useful for language and temporal data.
  • Can model nonlinear relationships.

Disadvantages

  • Vanishing gradient problem.
  • Training can be slow because of sequential computation.
  • Long-term dependencies can be difficult for basic RNNs.
  • Requires careful hyperparameter selection.
  • Larger models require significant computational resources.

Interesting Real-World Scenario

🚦 Predicting a Traffic Surge Before It Happens

Imagine a smart city receiving traffic measurements every five minutes.

At 5:00 PM:
2,000 vehicles/hour

At 5:05 PM:
2,300 vehicles/hour

At 5:10 PM:
2,700 vehicles/hour

At 5:15 PM:
3,100 vehicles/hour

An RNN can learn temporal patterns from historical traffic data. If similar patterns previously resulted in a severe congestion event, the system can forecast increasing traffic.

The traffic control system could then adjust signal timing, send route recommendations and alert commuters before the road becomes heavily congested.

When should we use RNN forecasting?

Good Fit

  • Sequential data
  • Large historical datasets
  • Nonlinear temporal patterns
  • Multiple interacting variables

Not Always Necessary

  • Very small datasets
  • Simple predictable trends
  • Problems where statistical models are sufficient
  • Situations requiring highly interpretable models
